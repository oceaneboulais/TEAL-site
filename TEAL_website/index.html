About the Lab

The Thode Environmental Acoustic Lab pioneers interdisciplinary approaches to marine ecosystem conservation through cutting-edge acoustic and visual monitoring technologies. Our research leverages advanced directional acoustics and vector sensor arrays to study cetacean behavior and population dynamics while assessing the impacts of anthropogenic noise on marine mammal communication and migration patterns. Led by Dr. Aaron Thode's expertise in passive acoustic monitoring of bowhead whales, gray whales, and humpback whales in Arctic and Pacific environments, the lab has developed sophisticated algorithms for real-time acoustic localization and automated detection systems that operate even in the presence of industrial seismic surveys and shipping noise.

Read more

Through collaborative research with graduate students and postdoctoral scholars, our work extends beyond cetacean conservation to encompass coral reef ecosystem enhancement through acoustic enrichment techniques. We employ autonomous visual monitoring systems combined with passive acoustics to track fish larvae settlement and reef fish recruitment, investigating how underwater soundscapes—both natural and artificially enriched—influence larval behavior and community assembly on degraded reefs. This multifaceted approach integrates bioacoustic analysis, computer vision, and oceanographic instrumentation to address critical questions in marine conservation, from understanding the Lombard effect in whale populations responding to noise pollution, to quantifying the efficacy of acoustic attraction methods for coral reef restoration, ultimately providing data-driven insights for policy makers and conservation practitioners working to protect vulnerable marine ecosystems in an era of increasing human impact.

Our Mission

To advance our understanding of marine ecosystems through innovative vector sensor-based acoustic research, autonomous monitoring with audio-visual modalities, and applied machine learning. Read more

Our work supports conservation by revealing how marine animals communicate, respond to environmental change, and interact with soundscapes in dynamic coastal and polar ecosystems. We pair field observations with signal processing, computer vision, and ecological analysis to develop practical tools for protecting vulnerable marine life and improving ocean stewardship.
